# Bridge — `claim_failed_deposit` (Sepolia ↔ Era Sepolia)

`claim_failed_deposit({ proof })` is zkSync's L1-side
recovery primitive for L1→L2 deposits whose L2 execution
reverted. The L1Nullifier verifies the supplied proof of
failure and credits the depositor back from the L1 escrow.

The flow:

1. **Submit a failing deposit** — `send_erc20` with
   `l2_gas_limit` deliberately set below the era-contracts
   `L1_TX_MIN_L2_GAS_BASE` (= 173_484). The L1→L2 priority
   op lands on L2 but reverts with out-of-gas, ending up in
   the failed-deposit queue.
2. **Wait for batch inclusion** — Era seals batches every
   few minutes on Sepolia; the failed op needs to be sealed
   before its merkle proof is fetchable.
3. **Assemble the proof** — off-band via two RPCs against
   `l2.reader`:
   - `zks_getL2ToL1LogProof(l2TxHash, logIndex)` → gives
     `batchNumber`, `id` (= `l2MessageIndex`), `proof`
     (= `merkleProof`).
   - `zks_getTransactionDetails(l2TxHash)` → gives
     `l2TxNumberInBatch`.
   The remaining fields (`chainIdNumeric`, `depositSender`,
   `l1Token`, `amount`, `l2TxHash`) come from the original
   deposit context. A future `fetch_failed_deposit_proof`
   helper will package this — for now the demo expects the
   pasted JSON.
4. **Claim on L1** — `claim_failed_deposit` signs
   `L1Nullifier.claimFailedDeposit(...)` on L1 with the
   9-field proof. The L1Nullifier walks the merkle tree
   anchored at the sealed batch's root and unlocks the
   escrow.

**Naming.** zkSync calls the proof bundle a
`FailedDepositProof` — distinct from the withdraw-side
`MessageProof` (slice 4c) because the failed-deposit case
also carries the `(depositSender, l1Token, amount)` trio
that the Nullifier needs to credit the L1 refund. Both
compose `zks_getL2ToL1LogProof`, but their wire shapes are
different.
