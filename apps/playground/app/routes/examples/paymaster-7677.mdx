## The two-call pattern

`pm_getPaymasterStubData` and `pm_getPaymasterData` exist
because the v0.7 userOpHash depends on the paymaster
fields, and the bundler's `eth_estimateUserOperationGas`
needs realistic gas limits to estimate against. The flow
splits in two:

1. **Stub.** Paymaster returns dummy `paymaster` +
   `paymasterData` (same shape and length as the real
   signature) plus realistic
   `paymasterVerificationGasLimit` and
   `paymasterPostOpGasLimit`. Bundler estimates against
   these; client signs nothing yet.
2. **Final.** After estimation, the client asks for the
   real `paymaster` + `paymasterData`. The paymaster
   has now committed to sponsoring the call against the
   final UserOp shape. The client signs the userOpHash
   over this shape and submits.

If the spec had a single call, the round-trip would
require the paymaster to sign before the client knew the
final gas — circular dependency. The split breaks the
cycle.

## v0.6 vs v0.7

ERC-7677 supports both EntryPoint versions. The schemas
in `@ethernauta/erc/7677/paymaster` carry both variants
behind a union; v0.7 is first so it wins on ambiguous
payloads (its required-key set is a strict superset of
v0.6's). `apply_to_user_op` only handles v0.7 responses —
v0.6 carries a single packed `paymasterAndData` blob that
has no slot in the v0.7 `UserOperation` schema, so the
helper throws a typed error pointing v0.6 callers at
manual composition.

## Composing with `@ethernauta/eip/4337`

The `apply_to_user_op` output is just a `UserOperation`
with the paymaster fields populated. The next steps —
`eth_estimateUserOperationGas`, `get_user_op_hash`,
`sign_user_op`, `eth_sendUserOperation` — come from
`@ethernauta/eip/4337` and don't know or care that a
paymaster was involved. See the{" "}
